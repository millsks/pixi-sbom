What it isn't

Not a vulnerability scanner

It tells you what you're shipping and hands that to whatever scans it. It doesn't replace the security tooling we already pay for.

Not a policy engine for the whole company

It enforces license and vulnerability rules for one build. Governance across every team is a different problem with different owners.

Not a promise that nothing is missing

When an upstream publishes no license or no advisory, the document says so instead of filling in a guess. You can tell an unanswered question from an empty answer.

Take your time on this slide. It's what makes the rest of the deck believable, and anyone in the room who has bought tooling before is waiting to see whether you'll admit to limits. Put the third one in your own words: the document separates "we asked and there was nothing" from "we couldn't ask", and that's what makes it usable as evidence rather than a claim. If someone pushes on overlap with a scanner we already have, agree with them — this feeds that scanner a complete inventory it doesn't have today for our conda packages.
