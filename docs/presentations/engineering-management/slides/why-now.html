The two financial rules already apply. Neither says "SBOM"; an SBOM is how we show the inventory they require.

Sources: PCI DSS v4.0 Req. 6.3.2 · Commission Delegated Reg. (EU) 2024/1774 Art. 10(2)(d) · Reg. (EU) 2024/2847 · EO 14028

The compliance people in this room know these rules better than you do, so state them plainly and don't stretch them. PCI DSS 4.0 Requirement 6.3.2: maintain an inventory of bespoke and custom software, and of the third-party software components built into it, to support vulnerability and patch management. It was a best practice until 31 March 2025 and is assessed from then on. It covers any organization that stores, processes or transmits cardholder data. DORA: Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1774, Article 10(2)(d), requires financial entities to track the usage of third-party libraries, including open-source libraries, used by ICT services that support critical or important functions, and to monitor their versions and updates. It also covers software the entity develops itself. DORA has applied since 17 January 2025. Cyber Resilience Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/2847: vulnerability reporting applies from 11 September 2026; the SBOM and the other main obligations from 11 December 2027. Neither financial rule names SBOMs. Say that if asked: an SBOM is the auditable, machine-readable form of the inventory both require, not a separate obligation. Not on the slide on purpose: NYDFS Part 500.13 (asset inventory of information systems, from 1 November 2025) and the 2023 US interagency guidance on third-party risk. Both are relevant to a bank, but neither asks for a component-level inventory. If someone asks whether we're in scope today: for card data and EU financial entities, yes, already. Customers are asking either way.
